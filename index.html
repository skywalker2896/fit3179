Beating the Odds at Australian Open Does ranking predicts who wins? From draw to the final point

The Australian Open is the first of the four Grand Slams, held every January in Melbourne Park. Starting with qualifying and finishing with the men's and women's finals, it spans about three weeks and draws record crowds, making it one of the most attended sports events in Australia.

Each year, 256 players enter the 2 singles main draw, 32 seeded players each according to their world ranking. In theory, seeding protects the favourites from leaving early but in practice, upsets happen and that tension to whether ranking predicts who wins.

Section 3 · Does Ranking Predict Performance

Figure 05 · Heatmap Matrix

Players Expected vs Actual Exit Round

Source: Tennis My Life

How to read this

Each player is placed in the column for the round their seed predicts they will reach (seeds 1–4: SF, 5–8: QF, 9–16: R16, 17–32: R32, unseeded: R1/R2) and the row for the round they actually exited.

Rankings set expectations, but tournament results tell a different story. Did the favourites live up to expectations? The diagonal represents players who performed as expected; cells away from the diagonal reveal early exits and unexpected runs.

A player's ranking usually reflects their standings in the tournament, but how closely does it correspond with on court time? The scatter plot explores the number of total games played by each player including unseeded and wild card players. Although we would expect the number 1 seed to play the most games since they are the topic to win the tournament, a highly ranked player is most likely to win in straight sets in the first 3-4 rounds.
